02 · open nights, twelve places

Twelve people a night, so everyone gets the eyepiece for as long as they want it.

Fridays and Saturdays, dusk until the moon interferes. Clouds roll you over to the next clear night for free — the sky decides, not the calendar.

We reply the same evening. If the cloud wins, your booking rolls to the next clear night — nothing to rebook, nothing to pay. Each night stops at twelve; book a full one and you hold the first place freed.

The fell

03 · getting here after dark

✦ Getting here

Forty minutes from the valley road, the last mile a farm track. Sat-nav gives up at the gate; the white posts don't.

Headlights off past the gate — parking lights carry you the last two hundred yards, and your eyes do the rest. 54.5°N 3.1°W, 412 m.

✦ Bring

  • COAT — more than the valley needs; the dome is cold on purpose, glass hates warm air
  • BOOTS — the path is a sheep track, not a pavement
  • FLASK — we refill it, all night, no charge
  • NOTHING ELSE — torches are ours to lend, and they are red

✦ The code

  • Red torches only past the gate — one white beam undoes the room's hour of dark
  • Phones face down; the screen you check is the sky you lose
  • Eyes take twenty minutes to open properly. Give them the twenty minutes.

The sky here measures Bortle 2 — dark enough that the milky way casts a shadow on a good night, and dark because everyone who comes up keeps it that way.

School nights

04 · term time, dark by teatime

In winter term it is properly dark before bedtime — a class can watch Saturn rise and still be home by eight. TUESDAY TO THURSDAY · 18:00–20:00 · IN TERM

Twelve at the eyepiece at a time, thirty in the warm room, and we rotate — nobody queues in the cold. The same chairs, the same Saturn, an hour earlier. Free for schools in the valley. The farm track turns a minibus at the gate, and the risk-assessment pack comes with our reply.

Write to dome@hollowfell.org with a first-choice week in term. We answer with the first clear night in it — the sky decides the date, the same deal the grown-ups get.

Journal

05 · notes from the dome

18 November

Andromeda, naked eye

A school group of nine-year-olds found it before the telescope did. It is two and a half million years away, and they were unimpressed it wasn't further.

2 November

The core, before it sets

Last look at the galactic centre this year. The dust lanes were the best they have been all season — the photograph is straight off the small camera.

27 October

In defence of the moon

We complain about it all year, then it fills the eyepiece and the room goes quiet. Full moon nights are the easiest ones to book — this is a hint.
